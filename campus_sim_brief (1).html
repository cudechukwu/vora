01 / Thesis
What we're actually building.
Read: 4 min
Status: Live draft
Audience: Internal
Author: Team

Modern apps killed ambient social life. Feeds replaced rooms. Notifications replaced footsteps. The result: a generation that's hyper-connected and chronically lonely, especially on college campuses where physical proximity should have made this impossible.

CAMPUS is a live multiplayer social simulation of a real university. Students enter as avatars, spawn in their dorms, walk to real-feeling locations, encounter friends, attend events, and exist socially — even when nothing important is happening. The gameplay is life rhythm.

It is not a dating app. It is not social media. It is not a game in the traditional sense. It is a digital social layer for real life, and the only metric that matters is whether students log in when they have nothing to do.

The world must support passive social existence. Players should enjoy being there even when doing nothing important.
// First Principle 01

Presence over content.

Other people existing nearby is the product. Posts, content, and feeds are secondary at best, hostile at worst.

// First Principle 02

Place is identity.

Foss, Beckham, Usdan, Olin — campus geography becomes shorthand for who you are, what you're doing, who you'll see.

// First Principle 03

Soft reputation, not metrics.

No followers, no likes, no scores. People become known for fashion, music taste, parties, presence — emergently, not algorithmically.

// First Principle 04

Never feel optimized.

No XP grinding, no daily rewards, no streaks. The moment players sense the casino, the spell breaks. Engagement comes from emotion, not from skinner boxes.

02 / Market
Who else is in this room.
Adjacent: YikYak
Adjacent: Fizz
Adjacent: Sidechat
Distant: Roblox
Distant: Zepeto
Distant: VRChat

The campus-social space is dominated by anonymous text apps (Fizz, Sidechat, Yik Yak revival) — feeds for gossip, missed connections, and shitposts. They solve signaling; they do not solve presence. They are read alone, in bed, late at night.

The metaverse/virtual world space (Roblox, Zepeto, VRChat) solves presence but at a cost: the world is fantasy, the friends are strangers, and the social capital you build there does not transfer to your real life. The lobby is global and meaningless.

CAMPUS sits in the gap: real social graph, virtual ambient presence. The people are people you'll see tomorrow in class. The buildings are buildings you walked through this afternoon.

The full vision is a ten-year project. The first product is not. The plan below assumes a 4–6 person core team, shipping a playable single-campus build in 12 months and reaching meaningful daily-life retention by month 18.

  • Greybox of Foss + a single dorm
  • Avatar prototype: outfit slot system, sit/walk/idle

04 / Go-To-Market
Campus by campus, in waves.
Method: Dense → Spread
Order: Small NESCAC →
→ Mid Privates →
→ State flagship pilot →
→ HBCU partner →
→ Coastal megas

The mistake everyone makes with campus social is launching everywhere at once. The product needs density to feel alive, and density only comes from one campus at a time, deeply, before moving on. Each wave is selected for a reason.

Wave 0 — Anchor
Fall '26
A small, weird, walkable campus where the founders are known and the social fabric is already dense.
Wesleyan
Why first: ~3,000 undergrads. Walkable. Strong arts/music culture aligned with founder narrative. Existing relationships across friend groups. Failure mode is contained; success is visible.
Wave 1 — NESCAC twins
Spring → Fall '27
Schools that share Wesleyan's scale, culture, and student archetype. Word-of-mouth carries naturally between them.
Amherst Williams Bowdoin Middlebury Hamilton
Why next: Identical population profile to anchor. Inter-campus dating, friendships, and rivalries already exist offline. Cross-campus FOMO accelerates organic spread without us paying for it.
Wave 2 — Mid privates
Spring '28
Stress-test the engine on bigger student bodies (4–8k) while staying in cultural range. First real density test.
Brown Vassar Tufts Oberlin Bard
Why now: Larger graphs reveal moderation, server, and matchmaking issues that won't appear at NESCAC scale. Brown is the lever — if it works there, Ivy-adjacent spread is unlocked.
Wave 3 — Cultural anchors
Fall '28
Specific schools whose culture exports nationally. Get in, and the brand becomes legible to every other campus.
Howard Spelman NYU Tisch USC Morehouse
Why now: Brand legitimacy upgrade. These campuses set the aesthetic ceiling for the rest of the country. HBCU partnership is non-negotiable — and earned, not extracted. Local partnerships, local ambassadors, local concerts.
Wave 4 — State flagships
Spring '29
Test what happens when "campus" is 40,000 people. Either we shard by college / dorm cluster, or the proximity model needs to evolve.
Michigan UT Austin UNC UW Madison
Why now: Truly different product problem. Greek life, megaclasses, sprawl. Likely requires "neighborhood" model — sub-campuses inside campuses. Treat as R&D wave, not pure growth wave.
Wave 5 — Global
'29 → '30
Once the U.S. template is tight, port it. Universities abroad have the same need with even less existing infrastructure.
McGill Oxford Edinburgh Trinity Dublin UCT
Why now: International student diasporas already pull friendships across borders. Cross-instance social features (visiting a friend's campus) become the global-network play. International rights, payments, and content law become real work.

Tactics, on every campus: No paid digital ads, ever. Launch loop is poster-based, busker-coded, in-person. Student ambassadors get a real stipend (not equity-as-payment). Launch event is a free in-person concert at a local venue tied to the campus, where the only way to RSVP is to be on the app.

What we will not do: growth hacks that compromise the feel of the world. No "invite 5 friends to unlock a hat." No referral leaderboards. The product is its own marketing — if it isn't, more posters won't fix it.

05 / Metrics
What we actually measure.
Anti-metric: Time spent
Anti-metric: Streaks
Anti-metric: DAU vanity
—
Real metric: Ambient open
Real metric: Co-presence
Real metric: Memory depth

Standard engagement metrics will lie to us about whether this works. A user opening the app to scroll for 40 minutes is not the win — a user opening it for 90 seconds because they had nothing to do, said hi to two people, and closed it, is. We measure for the kind of behavior we want to cause, not the kind that's easy to count.

// Ambient Open Rate
≥4x/day
Sessions opened without a notification or plan. The "what's everyone doing" check-in.
// Co-Presence Minutes
12min
Median minutes spent in the same space as ≥1 friend per session, not necessarily messaging.
// D30 Retention
55%
Target at full-campus launch. Below 40% means the world doesn't feel alive enough yet.
// Campus Penetration
30%
% of enrolled students active weekly. Below this, density collapses and the app feels empty.
// Outfit Changes / Week
5+
Proxy for identity investment. People who change outfits stay; people who don't, churn.
// IRL Spillover
42%
% of users who, monthly, met someone in-app and then in real life. This is the actual product.
// Memory Recalls
3/wk
Times per week a user views/shares a "memory" — a recorded moment, walk, or photo.
// NPS
60+
Standard, but real on a closed campus with no incentive to lie. Below 40 means we've built the wrong thing.
06 / Risk Register
What actually kills this.
Reviewed: 14.05.26
Owner: Founding team
Cadence: Quarterly
R/01
Density collapse High

If too few students are online, the world feels empty and the magic evaporates. Cold-start kills social products faster than anything.

Mitigation Launch one campus at a time with engineered concentration: invite-clusters by friend group, scheduled in-world events, and physical posters that pull people on at the same time. Never open before D7 retention hits target at closed alpha.
R/02
Moderation / safety High

Real-name campus social is a harassment, stalking, and doxxing surface. Romance + minors-adjacent + proximity = serious obligations.

Mitigation Edu-gating + photo verification at signup. Block/report flows are first-class UI, not buried. Dedicated T&S lead by P03. Crush system is private-by-default. No location sharing outside the in-world map. External legal review pre-launch.
R/03
Optimization-creep High

Pressure to add streaks, XP, daily rewards, and "engagement features" will be intense. Adding them will quietly kill the soul of the product, slowly, before anyone admits it.

Mitigation Founders document a written "no-list" at the start. Investor decks state explicitly that we are not chasing screen time. Anti-metrics tracked publicly inside the team. Quarterly review for drift.
R/04
Build complexity High

Multiplayer + real-time + avatars + voice + physics-feeling movement is genuinely hard. Studios spend $50M+ on less. Founder concern was correctly "10 years" — that's the maximal version.

Mitigation Aggressive scope cuts at P01–P02. 2D-isometric or low-poly 3D, never photorealistic. ReadyPlayerMe-style avatar pipeline. Photon / managed multiplayer stack. Treat the engine choice as the most important hire-or-buy decision in the company.
R/05
University legal pushback Med

Universities may object to virtual replicas of their buildings, mascots, or trademarks. Especially if anything embarrassing happens on the platform that traces back to the brand.

Mitigation Stylized — never literal — building names and architecture. Friendly student-org partnerships before launch. Pre-emptive outreach to student affairs. Optional university partnership tier later, opt-in.
R/06
Semester seasonality Med

Summer and breaks empty the campus. A campus-only product has a brutal 3-month annual hibernation, repeated forever.

Mitigation Multi-campus rollout staggers seasons. International campuses on different calendars. Long-arc product (situation simulator, see §07) is the off-season hedge — the platform isn't only campus forever.
R/07
Copycat from a major Med

Snap, Meta, Discord, or Roblox could clone the core loop in six months once it works.

Mitigation Speed and brand. The defensibility is not the tech; it's that we are the team that *belongs there.* Busker-coded, student-coded, real. Bigcos can't fake that, and trying to looks worse than not trying.
07 / Horizon
Where this all leads.
Frame: Long arc
Term: 5–10 years
Status: Direction, not plan

The original idea — phrased in the team's earlier conversation as "the coolest video game that needs to be created" — was a situation simulator: a place where anyone could simulate any situation, real or imagined. A club in Berlin. NFL GM on draft night. Wall Street on a Monday morning after a war breaks out. The reaction at the time was: incredible idea, ten years to build.

That estimate is right. But the path to that horizon runs through the campus product, not around it. A working campus sim is, structurally, a working situation sim with one specific situation pre-built. Every system we ship at Wesleyan — proximity, avatars, ambient density, event spawning, memory, social energy — is a brick in the larger thing.

The campus is just the first situation. Every situation worth being in is on the same engine.

// HZN.01

Years 1–3 — The Campus Engine

Prove the model on one university type. Build the systems — presence, ambient, memory — that make any social simulation feel real. Ship 10–20 campuses.

// HZN.02

Years 3–6 — Adjacent Worlds

Adapt the engine to non-campus situations with similar shape: cities, music scenes, conferences, festivals, summer programs. Same loop, different geography.

// HZN.03

Years 6–10 — The Situation Sim

Open the engine. Let users — or operators — spawn arbitrary scenarios: trading desks, club nights in Berlin, draft rooms, kitchens at dinner rush. Practice. Rehearse. Live in.

The team was right that a situation simulator built from a cold start would take a decade and probably never ship. But that is not the bet on the table. The bet is: build one small, real, beloved situation first. Earn the right to build the next. Let the engine become general by the time anyone notices.

That's not a video game project. That's a company.

Existing socially / is the gameplay. Everything else is just where it happens.
